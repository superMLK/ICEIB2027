\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Given a natural-language message $M$, health metrics $H$, a set of declared
allergens $A$, a location $\ell$, a meal time $t$, and today's activity $x$,
NutriAgent selects the top-3 meals from the walkable set $\mathcal{M}(\ell)$
and replies in natural language, subject to four requirements:
(i)~\emph{safety}---every recommended meal satisfies all clinical and allergen
hard constraints, and no LLM output can change this;
(ii)~\emph{numeric grounding}---every calorie, protein, sodium, price, and
distance value in the reply matches the database;
(iii)~\emph{nutritional fit and personalization}---meals approach an
activity-aware meal budget and follow preferences such as ``something light'';
and (iv)~\emph{resilience}---a safe recommendation is still produced when the
LLM is unavailable.

\subsection{Design Principles}
Five principles distilled from the clinical and behavioral literature shape the
design. \emph{Safety is non-negotiable}: clinical contraindications and
allergens are hard boundaries, not objectives to trade against
preference~\cite{mohw2018allergen,yan2025ogsrl,vasantlal2026morla2c}.
\emph{Pattern and quality over calories}: guidelines judge sodium, glycemic
load, saturated fat, and potassium, not energy
alone~\cite{jones2025hypertension,ada2026standards,kdigo2024ckd}.
\emph{Adherence beats the theoretical optimum}: comparable diets yield similar
outcomes, so the advice a user actually follows matters
most~\cite{gardner2018dietfits,thaler2008nudge}.
\emph{Energy balance is dynamic and noisy}: needs vary with activity, but
wrist-worn energy estimates carry errors above
30\%~\cite{hall2011energy,germini2022wearable,shcherbina2017wrist}.
\emph{Context defines feasibility}: advice must fit the time, place, and form in
which a user can act~\cite{lei2017actorcritic,svihrova2025bandits,lee2025pearl}.

\subsection{System Architecture}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the architecture. A user asks in a chat
interface (1); the message and the sidebar profile form the query (2). The
Planner (3) parses the intent (recommend, feedback, or explain a rejection),
overrides the location, meal time, and activity mentioned in the chat, and calls
the tools in order. The data module (4) obtains activity data through an IoT
adapter and retrieves nearby meals from the nutrition database (K1). The
analytics engine (5) computes the meal budget and an 8-dimensional state $s_t$.
The domain-knowledge gate (6) removes unsafe meals using the clinical rules (K2)
and allergen aliases (K3). The decision module (7) ranks the safe pool with a
local preference model and Gemini semantic scores, and the output module (8)
produces a grounded reply and records feedback in the interaction memory (K4).
The guiding split is that \emph{rules own safety and numbers, while the LLM
owns understanding and expression}.

\subsection{Activity-Aware Meal Budget}
The basal metabolic rate follows the Mifflin--St~Jeor
equation~\cite{mifflin1990bmr}. Because wearable energy estimates are
unreliable~\cite{germini2022wearable}, only a damped share of the active energy
$E_{\mathrm{act}}$ is added, and the budget of a meal is capped:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{TDEE} &= \mathrm{BMR} + 0.65\,E_{\mathrm{act}}, \label{eq:tdee}\\
    B_t &= \min\!\left(\rho_t \cdot \mathrm{TDEE},\ 900\ \mathrm{kcal}\right),
    \label{eq:budget}
\end{align}
where $\rho_t$ is the share of the daily energy assigned to meal time $t$.
Macronutrient targets are then adjusted by condition; for prediabetes, for
example, the split is 35\% carbohydrate, 35\% protein, and 30\% fat.
Before \eqref{eq:tdee} is applied, a cross-sensor plausibility check runs: more
than 10{,}000 steps with a mean heart rate of at most 70\,bpm is treated as
vehicle vibration and corrected, and if the wearable data cannot be obtained, a
static estimate is used and the budget is flagged as low confidence
(LOW\_CONF).

Two examples illustrate the effect. After a marathon, a watch may report
1{,}800\,kcal of activity; adding it one-to-one would push the dinner budget
toward an oversized, oily meal, whereas \eqref{eq:tdee} adds 1{,}170\,kcal to
the daily need and \eqref{eq:budget} still caps the dinner at 900\,kcal. When a
phone vibrating in a car records 12{,}000 extra steps within an hour while the
heart rate stays low, the plausibility check flags the data instead of raising
the budget.

\subsection{Clinical and Allergen Gate}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate} filters the candidates before any LLM is involved.
Declared allergens are expanded through an alias table covering the 11
categories that Taiwan requires on food labels~\cite{mohw2018allergen}, so
declaring ``seafood'' excludes both fish and crustaceans. A chat message may add
allergens but never remove them. The sodium threshold of 700\,mg per meal is a
design decision derived from the WHO limit of 2{,}000\,mg per
day~\cite{who2025hypertension} divided over three meals and rounded up; it is
not a guideline value. The chronic kidney disease (CKD) rule is deliberately
more conservative than KDIGO 2024, which individualizes potassium
intake~\cite{kdigo2024ckd}, because the system has no serum potassium data. If
the safe pool is empty, the rules are never relaxed: the agent returns
NO\_COMPLIANT\_MEAL with suggestions on how to order more safely.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Clinical and Allergen Hard-Constraint Gate}
    \label{alg:gate}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require candidates $C$, health metrics $H$, allergens $A$
        \Ensure safe pool $S$, rejection log $V$
        \State $S \gets \emptyset$, $V \gets \emptyset$
        \State $\hat{A} \gets \bigcup_{a \in A} \mathrm{Alias}(a)$
            \Comment{TFDA-11}
        \For{each meal $m \in C$}
            \State $R \gets \emptyset$
            \If{$m.\mathit{allergens} \cap \hat{A} \neq \emptyset$}
                \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{Allergen}\}$
            \EndIf
            \If{$\mathit{SBP} \ge 130$ \textbf{or} $\mathit{DBP} \ge 85$}
                \If{$m.\mathit{Na} > 700$\,mg \textbf{or} $m$ is deep-fried}
                    \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{HTN}\}$
                \EndIf
            \EndIf
            \If{$\mathit{FPG} \ge 100$ \textbf{and}
                ($m.\mathit{GI}$ high \textbf{or} $m.\mathit{sugar} > 5$\,g)}
                \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{Prediabetes}\}$
            \EndIf
            \If{$\mathit{UA} \ge 7.0$ \textbf{and} $m.\mathit{purine}$ high}
                \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{Gout}\}$
            \EndIf
            \If{$\mathit{LDL} \ge 130$ \textbf{and}
                ($m.\mathit{satfat} > 5$\,g \textbf{or} fried)}
                \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{LDL}\}$
            \EndIf
            \If{$\mathit{eGFR} < 60$ \textbf{and}
                ($m.\mathit{K}$ high \textbf{or} $m.\mathit{P}$ high)}
                \State $R \gets R \cup \{\textsc{CKD}\}$
            \EndIf
            \If{$R = \emptyset$}
                \State $S \gets S \cup \{m\}$
            \Else
                \State $V \gets V \cup \{(m, R)\}$
            \EndIf
        \EndFor
        \State \Return $S, V$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Hybrid Ranking and Grounded Reply}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:rank} ranks the safe pool $S$. A local preference score
$p_m \in [0,1]$ combines flavor weights, protein density, calorie closeness,
and keywords in $M$. When Gemini~2.5 Flash~\cite{gemini2025report} is
available, it returns a semantic score $g_m \in [0,1]$ for each meal in JSON
mode~\cite{google2026structured} at temperature~0.1. To reduce the position
bias of LLM rankers~\cite{hou2024llmrank}, the candidates are shuffled and
scored one by one rather than ranked. The scores are combined as
\begin{align}
    q_m &= 0.5\,p_m + 0.5\,g_m, \label{eq:hybrid}\\
    \mathrm{score}_m &= 0.50\,q_m + 0.35\,\mathrm{fit}_m
        - 0.15\min\!\left(1, d_m / 1.5\right), \label{eq:score}
\end{align}
where $q_m = p_m$ if no LLM score exists,
$\mathrm{fit}_m = 1 - (0.6\,e^{\mathrm{kcal}}_m + 0.4\,e^{\mathrm{prot}}_m)$
measures the calorie and protein error against the budget, and $d_m$ is the
walking distance in km.

The reply follows retrieval-augmented, tool-using
patterns~\cite{lewis2020rag,yao2023react}: Gemini receives the verified data
as JSON together with the last four turns, is instructed to quote every number
verbatim, and generates at temperature~0.25. A deterministic template reply is
always built first. The LLM reply replaces it only if every number it contains
matches the database; a timeout (12\,s), an API error, or any mismatch keeps
the template.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Hybrid LLM Ranking and Grounded Reply}
    \label{alg:rank}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require safe pool $S$, state $s_t$, budget $B_t$, message $M$,
            log $V$, history $\mathcal{H}$
        \Ensure top-3 meals $R$, reply $Y$
        \State $g \gets \emptyset$
        \If{LLM available \textbf{and} $M \neq \varnothing$}
            \State $g \gets \textsc{LLMScores}(\textsc{Shuffle}(S), B_t, M)$
        \EndIf
        \For{each meal $m \in S$}
            \State $p_m \gets \textsc{LocalPref}(s_t, m, M)$
            \State compute $q_m$ by \eqref{eq:hybrid} ($q_m \gets p_m$ if $m \notin g$)
            \State compute $\mathrm{score}_m$ by \eqref{eq:score}
        \EndFor
        \State $R \gets$ top-3 meals of $S$ by $\mathrm{score}$
        \State $Y \gets \textsc{TemplateReply}(R, H, B_t)$
        \Comment{numbers from DB}
        \If{LLM available}
            \State $Y' \gets \textsc{LLMReply}(\mathrm{JSON}(R, V, B_t), \mathcal{H}_{-4:})$
            \If{$Y' \neq \textsc{null}$ \textbf{and}
                $\mathrm{Num}(Y') \subseteq \mathrm{DB}(R, B_t)$}
                \State $Y \gets Y'$
            \EndIf
        \EndIf
        \State \Return $R, Y$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Online Preference Update}
The preference weights $w = (w_{\mathrm{light}}, w_{\mathrm{savory}},
w_{\mathrm{protein}})$ are updated from feedback $f$ on the top meal. The
reward and the update rule are
\begin{equation}
    r(f) =
    \begin{cases}
        +1.0, & f = \text{accept},\\
        -0.6, & f = \text{too oily},\\
        -0.2, & f = \text{skip},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
    \text{accept:}\quad   & w \gets w + 0.08\,\mathbf{1}_{a}, \nonumber\\
    \text{too oily:}\quad & w_{\mathrm{light}} \gets \min(1,\ w_{\mathrm{light}} + 0.12), \nonumber\\
                          & w_{\mathrm{savory}} \gets \max(0.1,\ w_{\mathrm{savory}} - 0.15), \nonumber\\
    \text{skip:}\quad     & w \gets \mathrm{clip}(0.95\,w,\ 0.1,\ 1), \label{eq:pref}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{1}_{a}$ marks the flavors of the accepted meal (and protein if
its protein ratio is at least 0.22). The transition $(s_t, a, r, s_{t+1}, f)$
is stored in the interaction memory. This is a heuristic with fixed step sizes,
not a gradient-trained model; it keeps the feedback--reward--update structure
of contextual bandits~\cite{lei2017actorcritic}, and the stored transitions
allow a learned bandit to be fitted offline later.

\subsection{Processing Flow}
Fig.~\ref{fig:flowchart} summarizes the agent loop. A feedback message such as
``too oily'' first updates $w$ by \eqref{eq:pref} and then triggers a new
recommendation. The exception paths keep the loop safe: missing wearable data
leads to a static estimate, fewer than four candidates within 1.2\,km expands
the search to 2.5\,km once, an empty safe pool returns NO\_COMPLIANT\_MEAL, an
unavailable LLM leaves only the local model, and a numeric mismatch falls back
to the template reply.
