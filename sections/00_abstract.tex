\begin{abstract}
More people now ask chatbots whether a meal suits their health, yet large
language models (LLMs) report nutrition numbers unreliably, which is risky for
people with hypertension, prediabetes, or kidney disease. This paper proposes
NutriAgent, a grounded conversational agent for eating-out decisions that
combines clinical nutrition rules, wearable IoT data, and Google Gemini. A
Planner parses intents from the chat, computes an activity-aware meal budget
with damped wearable energy, retrieves walkable meals, and applies a
deterministic clinical and allergen gate before any LLM call. A hybrid ranker
then combines a local preference model with Gemini scores on the safe pool
only, and every number in the LLM reply is checked against the database, with a
template reply as fallback. The evaluation design uses 50 synthetic clinical
profiles, at least 200 Taiwanese meals, a Chinese dialogue set, and five
baselines, including a Gemini zero-shot recommender. An offline functional
check of the prototype on 45 evaluations showed no clinical or allergen
violations, a calorie error of 114.48\,kcal, and sub-millisecond latency; we
expect the full evaluation to keep violations at zero with or without the
LLM.
\end{abstract}
